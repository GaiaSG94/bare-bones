01 · The dev team copies the skeleton.One command: pnpm create bare-bones. The skeleton has five extension points and reads in about an hour.
02 · The treasurer gets a raw bank export.The neighbourhood association exports its accounts as CSV: three date formats, commas and dots mixed up.
03 · The file is read in the browser.Dropped into Bare Bones, it is parsed in the background: encoding, dates, numbers. Nothing is sent to a server.
04 · Every column gets a type.Date, text, category, amount. Rows that fail to parse stay visible in a separate panel.
05 · Filters and charts build themselves.Amount ranges, categories, search: totals and charts update on every click, with no chart library.
06 · The whole view fits in one link.Filters, sort order and chart are encoded in the URL's #bb= fragment, which never reaches the server.
07 · Members open the same view.On their phones, with no account and nothing to install, they see exactly what the treasurer prepared.
08 · Same skeleton, a second app.The sports club plugs match results, the league table and form streaks into the same five extension points. Not one line of the skeleton changes.
09 · The league table, shared publicly.After each match day the club posts a short public #bb= link. Players and parents open the table on their phones, no account needed.

[ 01 — Overview ]

Drop a spreadsheet.
Share an explorer.

Bare Bones turns a plain CSV into a typed table, filters, charts and a share link. Everything happens in the browser: no server, no account.

9 steps · about 65 s
The problem

A community group is handed a spreadsheet. Nobody will open it, the usual tools are too heavy, and running a server is out of the question.

The answer — Bare Bones

A copy-ready data explorer that runs in the browser and is shared with a single link. Zero server, zero account.
